About the name

Why We're Called Carnegien Freedom

Fair question — and we'd rather answer it directly than dodge it.

The name comes from Andrew Carnegie's story: a poor immigrant who arrived in America with nothing, built one of the largest fortunes in history, and then spent his later years giving nearly all of it away — convinced that a life well-lived meant using what you'd earned to lift others, not just yourself. That arc — from struggle, to security, to genuine freedom to live and give on your own terms — is what we mean by "Carnegien freedom." It's not about becoming a millionaire. It's about every worker who has given their whole working life to a job reaching retirement with enough — financially and mentally — to actually be free. That's the freedom we think every retiring worker deserves, and it's the freedom this page exists to help you build toward.

To be completely clear: Carnegien Freedom is not affiliated with the Carnegie Foundation, Carnegie Mellon University, or any Carnegie-endorsed organization. We have no legal or institutional connection to that legacy, and we're not claiming one. We are an independent project, started in 2026 by Nantim Mullah Dadi, built around a set of values inspired by that story — nothing more.

If the name feels borrowed, that's a fair criticism, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. A name doesn't earn trust — content does. So judge us by what we publish here: is it accurate, is it useful, does it actually help you plan for a freer retirement? If it does, the name will have earned its place. If it doesn't, you should be skeptical of it — and of us.

We'd rather be questioned upfront than assumed legitimate by default. Thank you for asking.

Common Warning Signs

  • Guaranteed returns or unusually high pay for little effort.
  • Pressure to act immediately or keep an opportunity secret.
  • Requests for upfront fees, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or bank credentials.
  • Vague business details, unverifiable testimonials, or suspicious contact addresses.

Before You Commit

Search independently, verify the organization using trusted sources, read the terms, and ask someone you trust for a second opinion. A legitimate opportunity can withstand careful questions.

Remember: Legitimate employers and educators do not require payment to release promised earnings or secure a job.

Report and Protect Yourself

Stop communicating, preserve messages and receipts, contact your bank quickly if money was sent, and report suspected fraud to the relevant authorities in your country.
